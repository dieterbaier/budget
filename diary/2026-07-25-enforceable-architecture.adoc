== Day 2 — 25 July 2026: making the architecture enforceable

The architecture had been *described*. This was the day it became *checkable*:
ArchUnit fitness functions (ADR-013) and four constraints, CON-001 to CON-004 —
framework-free core, hexagonal boundaries, persistence isolation, plain Java.

Review pushback that changed the result: the adapter clause was not the strict
form, and saying so in the ADR was better than quietly pretending otherwise. A
fitness function that is weaker than the rule it claims to enforce is a
half-truth in the record, and the fix is to write down which one it is.

=== The brief that was not the brief

The frontend architecture style was opened the same day, and the opening was
wrong. The brief mentioned Atomic Design, axios, TanStack Query, UnoCSS and
React Router, and I began treating that list as settled. It was not — it
described a *customer's* project. The correction was blunt and useful:

[quote]
____
"'Atomic design', 'Screaming architecture' are buzzwords for me… every
dependency should be one for a reason."
____

That sentence became a standing rule. Every dependency in this repo now has to
justify itself against *this* project:

* *axios* lost to `fetch` — nothing in a single-user PWA needed interceptors or
  the payload.
* *Atomic Design* lost to a feature-first structure, which names what the app
  does rather than how big its components are.
* *React Router* was *deferred*. With one screen there was nothing to route. It
  arrived on day 4, when a second screen actually existed — and ADR-014 records
  the deferral, so the later adoption reads as the plan working rather than as a
  reversal.
* *TanStack Query* and *UnoCSS* survived, on their own arguments.

The general lesson is not about any of those libraries. It is that a stack named
in a brief is a *hypothesis about this project*, and inherited context is the
easiest kind to mistake for a decision.

=== One question at a time

The other process correction that day: I asked three grilling questions in one
batch. That is not grilling, it is an interrogation, and it is hard to answer —
the answers collide, and the least interesting question tends to get the most
attention. One question at a time from then on.
