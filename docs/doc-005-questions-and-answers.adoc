= Questions And Answers
:id: DOC-005-questions-and-answers

This is the central discussion document for the private Budget project. It
records open and answered questions and the decisions taken. Open questions are
picked up by the generator into the open-questions index for the architecture
decisions chapter.

== Decision Notes

The AI bootstrap seeded this repository from the toolkit's private-budget
example. On 2026-07-24 the owner reviewed the scope and made the first product
and technology decisions in a clarification session. The confirmed product is a
*private, single-user actuals tracker* that replaces the owner's Notion budget:

* Transactions are entered from bank statements. Cash is not tracked, except a
  `Barabhebung` (cash withdrawal) booking. The app analyses spending; it is not
  a full ledger and does not derive account balances.
* Master data: category groups, categories, three accounts (current account, a
  call-money reserve account for non-monthly fixed costs, a call-money savings
  account), fixed-cost definitions (amount, interval, anchor date), and a
  monthly income table.
* Core views: current monthly expenditure with an overspending signal against
  average income; spend per category group; account-reserve requirements; a
  cash runway to month end; net-worth tracking; and a nominal pension projection
  that yields the pension shortfall.
* Clients: an installable React PWA is the only v1 client and covers both
  desktop and mobile. Offline capture and multi-user sharing are out of scope.

The corresponding technology decisions are recorded in ADR-001 and ADR-003
through ADR-008. Content confirmed in that session is marked `reviewed`;
newly derived or still-uncertain content stays `draft`/`proposed`.

On 2026-07-29 the owner supplied screenshots of the Notion budget being replaced
and the domain model was specified against them in a second clarification
session. The screenshots hold real financial data and are deliberately not part
of this repository; what they established is recorded in Q-PO-005 through
Q-PO-010 below, and Q-PO-004 was corrected where the notes had been wrong.

One statement from that session governs the rest and is easy to lose in the
detail:

[quote]
____
The system is not an accounting system. It helps the owner keep an eye on
spending — that was the original intention — and the categories and groups make
the household book configurable enough to see, from real figures, what the money
goes on. That is what helps with planning.
____

Several decisions only make sense in that light. The application derives no
account balances, keeps no ledger, and does not reconcile itself: fixed-cost
payments are checked by eye against the bank statement rather than booked, a
payment date landing on a Sunday needs no correction, and account balances are
typed in. A ledger would have to be right; this has to be *useful*, and the owner
pays for the difference with a small amount of manual care.

[[q-arch-003]]
=== Q-ARCH-003: What is the identity source for a Transaction?

Answer: Open.

A `Transaction` is an entity and needs identity (two identical-looking expenses
on the same day are distinct). If the owner's bank export carries a stable
unique reference (an end-to-end/transaction id), use it as the natural key;
otherwise the domain generates a `TransactionId` (UUID). This depends on the
actual bank statement format and is decided when the edit/delete slice
introduces transaction identity. See xref:adr-009-domain-identity-and-ddd[ADR-009].

=== Q-OPS-003: Which always-on host runs the backend once multi-device is enabled?

Answer: Open.

== Answered Questions

[[q-po-001]]
=== Q-PO-001: Should multiple household members share one budget?

Answer: No. The application is single-user with no shared or multi-user data.

[[q-po-002]]
=== Q-PO-002: Is the confirmed product scope a private single-household budgeting app?

Answer: Yes, refined to a private *single-user actuals tracker* that replaces
Notion. Confirmed by the owner on 2026-07-24.

[[q-po-003]]
=== Q-PO-003: Are mobile capture plus backend sufficient for v1, or is a web UI in scope?

Answer: A web UI is in scope and is the primary client. It is delivered as an
installable PWA that also serves mobile use; native mobile apps are out of
scope. See xref:adr-005-web-client-as-pwa[ADR-005].

[[q-arch-001]]
=== Q-ARCH-001: Which technology stack is chosen for backend, mobile client, and datastore?

Answer: Spring Boot (Java 21) hexagonal backend, React + TypeScript + Vite PWA,
and PostgreSQL with JPA and Flyway. See xref:adr-004-backend-stack-and-architecture[ADR-004],
xref:adr-005-web-client-as-pwa[ADR-005], and xref:adr-006-relational-datastore[ADR-006].

[[q-arch-002]]
=== Q-ARCH-002: Are the three proposed ADRs the right initial decisions to pursue?

Answer: ADR-001 (API boundary) and ADR-003 (server-owned calculation) are
confirmed and accepted. ADR-002 (mobile local-first offline entry) is withdrawn
and removed because offline capture is out of scope. New decisions ADR-004
through ADR-008 record the stack, PWA client, datastore, deployment/auth, and
the manual account-balance model.

[[q-ops-001]]
=== Q-OPS-001: Which backup retention period is required?

Answer: The backup approach is decided — a nightly `pg_dump` plus an in-app
export endpoint so the owner is never locked in, with dump copies kept off the
application host. A concrete retention target (keep at least the last twelve
snapshots) is a configuration detail to refine during Increment 1, not an open
architecture question.

[[q-ops-002]]
=== Q-OPS-002: What is the hosting and deployment target for the backend?

Answer: The MVP runs locally (backend plus PostgreSQL) on a single device. When
multi-device use is wanted, backend and database move to a single always-on host
serving all clients over HTTPS. The direction is decided; provider selection is
tracked as Q-OPS-003. See xref:adr-007-single-user-deployment-and-auth[ADR-007].

[[q-po-004]]
=== Q-PO-004: What is the exact pension-projection formula?

Answer: The projection is computed in *nominal* terms. The owner enters an
estimated nominal return (the rate a safe investment would yield today). The
inflation rate is the average of Germany's historical annual inflation rates
from https://www.global-rates.com/de/inflation/historisch/[global-rates.com].
The expected monthly need in retirement is derived from current costs, filtered
by a *pension-relevance* flag carried on categories, individual costs, and fixed
costs (for example, a mortgage repayment is flagged not relevant). The owner also
tracks net worth (life insurance, house value, account balances, investment
portfolio) and the latest statutory pension figures; the projected need minus
tracked net worth yields the *pension shortfall*. The precise compounding
mechanics are refined during the pension increment.

Corrected on 2026-07-29 in three places:

* *Where the pension-relevance flag is decided.* The original answer listed
  categories, costs and fixed costs without saying which wins. The booking wins:
  a category carries the default and a transaction or fixed cost may override it.
  The owner's example is `Auto` — the category stays relevant because fuel is
  still a cost in retirement, while the car loan (a fixed cost that will be
  repaid by then) and a one-off spoiler purchase (a transaction) are each flagged
  not relevant on the booking itself.
* *Where the inflation rate comes from.* Not a rate the owner looks up and types
  in. The application stores the historical annual rates as entered data and
  computes the average itself, so the figure moves as the series grows.
* *How the monthly need in retirement is derived.* Notion offers a manual
  override of the computed figure; the owner does not want it carried over,
  because it only obscures where the number comes from. The need is
  `value of pension-relevant costs at retirement − expected statutory pension`,
  computed and not overridable.

The Notion pension figures were *not* used to reverse-engineer the formula. The
owner reports that the sheet's capital requirement carries a known calculation
error, and two of its figures could not be reconciled with any plausible formula
during analysis. The projection is specified from scratch in the pension
increment rather than reproduced.

[[q-po-005]]
=== Q-PO-005: At what level is spending classified?

Answer: Three levels. A *category group* holds *categories*, and a category holds
*purposes* (German `Verwendungszweck`). A purpose belongs to exactly one
category; a transaction and a fixed cost may carry one, optionally.

The purpose is what the money was actually for — `Fernwärme` under the category
`Nebenkosten`, `Streaming (Musik)` under `Lizenzen`. The owner wants to see what
a single purpose costs per month, fixed and variable together, which is why it
cannot be a free-text field: aggregation needs identity.

It stays optional so quick entry does not require inventing a purpose first; the
purpose view then carries an explicit "without purpose" bucket. Because a purpose
belongs to one category, its total always rolls up into that category and its
group without contradiction.

[[q-po-006]]
=== Q-PO-006: Does "costs per month" mean the current month or a long-run average?

Answer: A long-run average — the recorded bookings divided by a *tracking
window*, which runs from the first recorded booking to today and is measured
day-precise rather than in whole months.

This is the figure the owner reads, and it answers a structural question ("what
does this group cost me per month") rather than a momentary one ("what have I
spent so far this month"). Day-precise matters: a half-finished month
contributes half a month to the divisor and its bookings so far to the numerator,
so a partially elapsed month does not drag the average down.

Fixed costs enter the same figure as their amortized monthly portion. Variable
costs are the averaged bookings. The overspending signal compares that total
against average monthly income and therefore also states something structural.

This re-scopes what Increment 1 delivered: `MonthlyExpenditureCalculator`
computes the current calendar month and no longer produces the leading figure.

[[q-po-007]]
=== Q-PO-007: How does a fixed-cost payment enter the record?

Answer: It does not. Fixed costs are defined once and checked, never booked.

The owner recognizes fixed-cost debits on the bank statement and skips them when
transcribing; the definition already contributes its monthly portion, so booking
them as well would count them twice. What the definition needs instead is the
date of the *last payment*, maintained by hand — the field the model called
`anchorDate`, which is not an abstract anchor but a value that moves forward. The
next payment date follows from it, without business-day correction: a due date
landing on a Sunday is fine, because nothing is executed from it.

Two figures derive from that date. The *reserve requirement* is the number of
whole months elapsed since the last payment multiplied by the monthly portion,
and is zero for monthly costs — that is the money that must sit on the call-money
reserve account. The *due list* shows what is payable now, and the owner uses it
in both directions: to catch a payment forgotten while transcribing, and to
retire a fixed cost that no longer exists — a cancelled gym contract shows up as
a last payment older than its own interval.

The payment intervals in use are monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, yearly and
two-yearly. Payment method (direct debit, credit card, PayPal) is recorded for
information only and enters no calculation.

[[q-po-008]]
=== Q-PO-008: How are refunds recorded, and does the gross figure stay visible?

Answer: A refund stays what the glossary already says it is — a booking with a
negative amount in the same category. No separate concept.

The group view additionally shows the sum of the negative amounts as `refunds per
month`, so gross and net are both readable: health costs of 133 EUR per month
against 178 EUR of reimbursements read better than the net figure of −45 EUR
alone. Both are derived from the same bookings, so nothing can drift apart. A
group total may legitimately be negative, and no view may treat that as an error.

[[q-po-009]]
=== Q-PO-009: Do savings and pension contributions count as costs?

Answer: Yes, deliberately.

They are roughly a fifth of the owner's monthly outgoings, so the overspending
signal reports "spending exceeds income" while part of that spending builds
wealth. That is the intended reading: the question the signal answers is whether
income matches outgoings, and *saving too much* is a valid answer to it — one the
owner can act on immediately by saving less, at the price of slower growth in net
worth. Excluding savings would hide a lever rather than sharpen the signal.

[[q-po-010]]
=== Q-PO-010: How is uncertainty in net worth and the pension projection expressed?

Answer: As a fixed band, not as a configurable scenario model.

Net worth carries a minimum, a likely and a maximum value. The pension projection
is computed twice: once with inflation only, once with inflation plus an
estimated cost increase. The inflation rate is derived from the entered
historical series; the cost increase is a single estimated percentage.

A named, parameterized scenario concept was considered and rejected as more model
than the two cases justify.
