== Requirements Overview

**This is not an accounting system.** It exists so the owner can see where the
money goes and plan from real figures; it derives no balances, keeps no ledger,
and does not reconcile itself. That is a scope statement, not a shortcoming —
several requirements below are deliberately lax because of it, and the owner pays
the difference with a small amount of manual care. See
`doc-005-questions-and-answers.adoc`.

The owner records transactions from bank statements — each with a name, booking
date, amount, category, account, and type (expense, income, or transfer).
Refunds are entered as a negative expense in the same category, so a category or
group total may legitimately be negative. Spending is classified on three levels:
category groups hold categories, and categories hold *purposes* (German
`Verwendungszweck`) — what the money was actually for. A purpose belongs to
exactly one category and is optional on a booking. Three accounts are managed
(current account, a call-money reserve account for non-monthly fixed costs, and a
call-money savings account) whose balances are entered manually.

Fixed-cost definitions carry a payee, an amount per cycle, a payment interval
(monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, yearly, or two-yearly), a category, an optional
purpose, a payment method, and the date of the last payment. **Fixed-cost
payments are never booked as transactions** — they are checked against the bank
statement instead, because the definition already contributes its monthly
portion. From the last payment date follow the next payment date (without
business-day correction) and the reserve requirement. A monthly income table
holds salary and yields an average income for the overspending comparison.

Monthly cost figures are *long-run averages*: recorded bookings divided by a
tracking window that runs from the first booking to today, measured day-precise.
The figure answers what something costs per month in general, not what has been
spent so far this month.

In scope for the first increments:

* Bootstrap of accounts, category groups, categories, purposes, fixed costs, and
  balances.
* Transaction entry and editing.
* Monthly cost (averaged variable costs plus amortized fixed costs) with an
  overspending signal against average income. Savings and pension contributions
  count as costs, deliberately.
* Spend per category group, per category, and per purpose — fixed and variable
  each, with refunds shown beside the net figure.
* Reserve requirement for the non-monthly-fixed-cost account, and a list of the
  fixed costs currently due.
* Cash runway from today to month end: remaining days at the variable daily rate,
  plus the fixed costs still due this month.
* Net-worth tracking (life insurance, house value, account balances, investment
  portfolio, and the latest statutory pension figures), each as a minimum, likely
  and maximum value.
* Pension projection (nominal), driven by a pension-relevance flag whose default
  sits on the category and whose override sits on the booking, computed twice —
  with inflation only and with inflation plus an estimated cost increase — and
  yielding the pension shortfall against tracked net worth. The inflation rate is
  averaged from an entered series of historical annual rates.

Out of scope: bank/API synchronization, cash-payment tracking (beyond a
`Barabhebung` booking), spending forecasts/planning, multi-user or shared
budgets, offline capture, financial advice, payment execution, and any
configurable scenario model beyond the fixed cases named above.
