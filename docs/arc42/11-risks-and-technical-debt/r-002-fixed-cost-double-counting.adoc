== R-002: Fixed Cost Counted Twice

=== Risk Statement

Because fixed costs contribute their amortized monthly portion from their
definition and are deliberately never booked (Q-PO-007), a fixed-cost debit that
the owner nevertheless transcribes from the bank statement is counted twice — and
nothing in the application can tell that it happened.

The duplicate is invisible by construction. A transcribed debit is a
well-formed booking in a plausible category; only the owner knows that a fixed
cost already covers it. The effect is silent inflation of the monthly cost
figure, the group total, and every figure derived from them — including the
overspending signal and the pension-relevant cost base.

This is the accepted cost of not booking fixed costs. The alternative — entering
every fixed-cost payment so the ledger is complete — was rejected by the owner as
work the application exists to save, and it would replace this risk with a
different one: forgetting an entry would understate costs instead.

=== Assessment

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Field | Value
| Likelihood | Medium (one lapse while transcribing).
| Impact | Medium (silent drift in every derived figure).
| Priority | Medium.
| Timeframe | From the first month of real use.
| Confidence | Medium (no usage data yet).
|===

Likelihood is medium because it takes a single lapse of attention and the entry
looks perfectly correct afterwards. Impact is medium because figures drift rather
than break — but every derived view inherits the error, and a wrong overspending
signal is the one thing the product exists to get right.

=== Mitigation Options

The mitigation is the workflow the owner already runs in Notion and wants in the
application: a *due fixed costs* list, reconciled by eye against the bank
statement.

* Show the fixed costs whose next payment has arrived, so a debit on the
  statement can be matched against a definition rather than transcribed.
* Flag a fixed cost whose last payment is older than its own interval. That
  catches both directions of the same problem — a payment the owner forgot to
  acknowledge, and a fixed cost that no longer exists at all, such as a
  cancelled contract still inflating the monthly figure.
* Held in reserve: a warning when a new booking resembles a due fixed cost by
  amount and payee. It would catch the duplicate at the moment it is made, but it
  needs matching rules nobody has asked for yet, and a system that is explicitly
  not an accounting system should not start guessing at reconciliation before
  there is evidence it must.

=== Decision or Acceptance

The owner accepted the risk on 2026-07-29 in favour of not entering fixed-cost
payments, and named the due-fixed-costs list as the control he relies on.

He confirmed the same day that the list alone is enough for now, and set the
condition under which that changes: *if this leads to repeated mistakes, a
duplicate-suspicion warning becomes worth building.* The trigger is real
recurrence in use, not a single slip — which is what makes it a decision rather
than a deferral.

The owner reviewed and released this risk as described on 2026-07-29, including
the assessment above.

=== Review Notes

Revisit the rating after the first months of real use. It rests on an estimate
