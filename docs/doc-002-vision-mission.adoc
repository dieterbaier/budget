[[vision-mission]]
= Vision And Mission
:id: DOC-002-vision-mission

Vision: Budget gives its owner a calm, self-hosted view of where the household
money actually goes, without handing personal spending data to a third party.

Mission: Replace the owner's Notion budget with a private, single-user
application that records real bank-statement transactions, keeps them organized
by category and category group, and answers a small set of concrete money
questions:

* How much am I spending this month, and is that more than my average income?
* For which group (house, car, dog, daughter, licensing, ...) do I spend most?
* How much must sit on each account, including the reserve account for
  non-monthly fixed costs?
* Am I on track for retirement given my current pot, costs, inflation, and
  expected return?

Second mission: serve as a validation testbed for the
architecture-knowledge-toolkit. The project is deliberately run by the toolkit's
own method — its skills, contracts, metamodel and templates — on real work rather
than on an example, to determine where the method helps, where it gets in the
way, and which gaps should be corrected upstream.

The word is *validate*, not *prove*. A testbed that can only confirm tells you
nothing; the useful outcomes so far have all been negative ones — a generator
behaviour the metamodel lacked, an overclaim about what a budget protected,
checks that passed while blind, and the absence of a rule for when an artifact is
not worth creating.

=== When the two missions disagree

They can disagree, and the resolution is not symmetric. Exercising the toolkit
adds documentation and review work; the product benefits from proportionate
effort and delivery.

*Product needs and architectural proportionality govern the application.* The
testbed observes and reports what real product work reveals. It must not add
architecture artifacts, constraints, dependencies or implementation complexity
solely to exercise the method.

The two also end differently. The product mission finishes when the owner stops
using Notion; the toolkit mission has no completion condition, which is precisely
why it needs a bound rather than a completion date. The bound is one question,
asked of every artifact: without it, would a later reader probably decide wrongly
or miss an important assumption? A finding can justify upstream toolkit work.
Artifact count justifies nothing.
